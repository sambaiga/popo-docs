# Key concepts

## Per unit

Voltages are reported **per unit** of each bus's own base voltage: 1.0 pu is nominal, whether the bus is
an 11 kV substation bus or a 230 V house. Statutory limits are expressed the same way, so a single number
compares across voltage levels.

## Phases

Low-voltage customers are usually connected to **one phase**. A European feeder spreads its single-phase
customers across all three phases; a North American one splits each house across the two legs of a
centre-tapped transformer. popo keeps track of which phase each customer is on: results label every bus by
the phase it is actually connected to, and rooftop arrays, chargers and batteries are installed on their
customer's own phase.

## Statutory limits

A statutory limit is a band together with a rule for applying it. EN 50160 asks that 95 % of ten-minute means over a week sit within
±10 % of nominal, so a network can hold a low voltage for an hour and still comply; ANSI C84.1 Range A is
±5 % with no such allowance. {py:class}`~popo.standards.VoltageLimits` carries both the band and the rule,
and a study selects a standard by name.

## Busbar and customers

Most networks are measured at the secondary substation's busbar. Voltage problems usually appear
elsewhere: rooftop solar raises voltage most at the far end of the longest service. A feeder can be within
limits at the busbar while its farthest customers are over the limit, so
{py:func}`~popo.powerflow.solve_time_series` can record every customer's voltage as well as the busbar's.

## Convergence

A time-series solve whose inverter control loop fails to converge still reports a solved power flow, with
meaningless reactive power. popo includes convergence in every result: a
{py:class}`~popo.powerflow.SolveReport` counts the steps that did not converge.

## Observability

A state estimator solves for a voltage magnitude and angle at every bus, less one reference angle:
$2N - 1$ unknowns for $N$ buses. Each measurement supplies one equation, and each bus known to inject
nothing supplies two. With fewer equations than unknowns no estimate exists, whichever solver is used.
{py:func}`~popo.estimation.observability` counts both before an estimate is attempted.
