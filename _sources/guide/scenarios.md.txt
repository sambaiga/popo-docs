# Scenarios

{py:mod}`popo.scenarios` builds the counterfactual networks a study compares: customers given real demand
profiles, and a share of them given rooftop solar, an EV charger or a battery.

## Profiles and penetration

- {py:func}`~popo.scenarios.assign_household_profiles` gives each existing load its own demand shape, drawn
  from a pool of real household profiles.
- {py:func}`~popo.scenarios.attach_generation` fits rooftop arrays to a fraction of customers, sized by
  {py:class}`~popo.scenarios.ArraySizing`.
- {py:func}`~popo.scenarios.attach_charging` gives a fraction of customers a charger, each driven by its own
  recorded session, so the diversified sum at the substation reflects real coincidence.

Penetration is the fraction of customers holding the technology. Every installation connects on its
customer's own phase ({py:func}`~popo.scenarios.service_connection`), unless a phase is forced with
`connection=`.

## Mitigation

| Lever | How | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Volt-VAr | `attach_generation(volt_var=VoltVarCurve(...))` | reactive power, adding to transformer loading |
| Volt-Watt | `attach_generation(volt_watt=VoltWattCurve.ieee_1547_default())` | curtailed generation |
| Storage | `attach_storage(dss, customers, ..., dispatch=StorageDispatch())` | a battery per customer |

OpenDSS gives each inverter one control, so Volt-VAr and Volt-Watt are installed one at a time.
{py:class}`~popo.scenarios.StorageDispatch` charges through the middle of the day and discharges in the
evening so a battery starts the next day empty; a schedule shared by every battery concentrates their
charging and discharging in time, which the second tutorial shows moving the over-voltage into the evening.
