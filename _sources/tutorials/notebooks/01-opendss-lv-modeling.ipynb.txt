    "# OpenDSS LV Modeling with popo\n",
    "\n",
    "## Objectives\n",
    "\n",
    "<p style=\"text-align: justify\">This notebook is a guide to modeling a low-voltage (LV) distribution network and running a power flow, the foundational skill most of <code>popo</code>'s other tooling builds on. By the end you will be able to:</p>\n",
    "\n",
    "<ul>\n",
    "<li style=\"text-align: justify\">Model an LV network in OpenDSS: a transformer, lines, and single-phase house loads.</li>\n",
    "<li style=\"text-align: justify\">Read the circuit's elements (lines, loads, transformers) back as records with <code>popo.network.Circuit</code>.</li>\n",
    "<li style=\"text-align: justify\">Extract power, current, voltage and loss results as tables with <code>popo.powerflow.results</code>.</li>\n",
    "<li style=\"text-align: justify\">Check bus voltages against a statutory limit, and list the readings that break it, with <code>popo.standards.VoltageLimits</code>.</li>\n",
    "<li style=\"text-align: justify\">Read a transformer definition file with <code>popo.network.read_transformers</code>.</li>\n",
    "<li style=\"text-align: justify\">Visualize the network's topology and work through three expansion exercises.</li>\n",
    "</ul>\n",
    "\n",

    "### Checking bus voltages against a statutory limit\n",
    "\n",
    "<p style=\"text-align: justify\">This is where <code>popo.standards.VoltageLimits</code> replaces doing the comparison by hand. Rather than eyeballing each <code>vmag_pu</code> against a hard-coded band, the per-unit voltages are judged against a named standard and the verdict carries <em>why</em> it passed or failed, not just a bare true/false. EN 50160 is the right choice here: a European LV network, not a North American one, so ANSI C84.1's narrower band would be a category error, not a more conservative check.</p>\n",
    "\n",
    "<p style=\"text-align: justify\">The verdict says whether the network complies; <code>violations</code> says where it does not, listing each reading outside the band with the side it falls on and how far past the bound it sits. The source bus is excluded first: it is held at its setpoint by construction, so it says nothing about what a customer sees.</p>"
   ]

    "## Summary\n",
    "\n",
    "<ul>\n",
    "<li style=\"text-align: justify\">A circuit is built from plain <code>dss.Text.Command(...)</code> calls: transformer, linecodes, lines, loads, in that order, then <code>controlmode</code>/<code>mode</code>/<code>VoltageBases</code> before solving.</li>\n",
    "<li style=\"text-align: justify\"><code>popo.network.Circuit</code> reads the compiled circuit back as records, and its transformers as the same <code>Transformer</code> record <code>read_transformers</code> produces from a definition file.</li>\n",
    "<li style=\"text-align: justify\"><code>popo.powerflow.results</code> reads a solved circuit back as long tables, one row per (element, phase), with each bus labelled by the phase it is actually on.</li>\n",
    "<li style=\"text-align: justify\"><code>VoltageLimits</code> judges those tables against a named standard: <code>check</code> for the verdict, <code>violations</code> for the readings that break it and by how much.</li>\n",
    "<li style=\"text-align: justify\">OpenDSS stays one call away throughout, through <code>circuit.command</code> or the <code>dss</code> module itself, for anything <code>popo</code> does not cover.</li>\n",
