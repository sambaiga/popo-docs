Network#

popo.network covers the circuit as defined: buses, lines, transformers, loads and the topology that connects them. Solving it is Power flow.

A handle on the compiled circuit#

Circuit is a view of whatever is compiled in the OpenDSS engine. It does not replace opendssdirect: circuits are still built with OpenDSS commands, through command() or the module itself.

from popo.network import Circuit

circuit = Circuit.load("feeder/Master.dss")    # Clear, Redirect and solve
lines = pd.DataFrame(circuit.lines)              # one record per line
transformers = list(circuit.transformers)        # popo.network.Transformer records
circuit.command("? Line.A_B.length")             # anything not covered by a record

OpenDSS runs one circuit per process, so a Circuit always sees the circuit compiled now: compiling another changes what every handle sees.

Transformers and customers#

  • read_transformers() reads a Transformers.dss definition file into a frame, keeping the nameplate and normal thermal ratings distinct. Circuit.transformers yields the same records from a live circuit.

  • residential_loads() lists a feeder’s low-voltage residential loads as (load name, bus) pairs, the form every scenario function takes.

Choosing a feeder on evidence#

  • profile_circuit() describes a circuit at its defined loading: customers, buses, rating, single-phase share and voltage range.

  • measure_sensitivity() raises the loading and re-solves, measuring how far the worst bus voltage moves per megawatt: the quantity that decides whether a voltage constraint binds.

  • screen() steps a large feeder briefly to find the transformers that actually bind, and select_by_utilisation() picks the ones worth monitoring.
