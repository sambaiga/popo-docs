Key concepts#

Per unit#

Voltages are reported per unit of each bus’s own base voltage: 1.0 pu is nominal, whether the bus is an 11 kV substation bus or a 230 V house. Statutory limits are expressed the same way, so a single number compares across voltage levels.

Phases#

Low-voltage customers are usually connected to one phase. A European feeder spreads its single-phase customers across all three phases; a North American one splits each house across the two legs of a centre-tapped transformer. popo keeps track of which phase each customer is on: results label every bus by the phase it is actually connected to, and rooftop arrays, chargers and batteries are installed on their customer’s own phase.

Statutory limits#

A limit is a rule, not a number. EN 50160 asks that 95 % of ten-minute means over a week sit within ±10 % of nominal, so a network can hold a low voltage for an hour and still comply; ANSI C84.1 Range A is ±5 % with no such allowance. VoltageLimits carries both the band and the rule, and a study selects a standard by name.

Busbar and customers#

A secondary substation’s busbar is where most networks are measured. It is not where voltage problems appear: rooftop solar raises voltage furthest from the substation, at the end of the longest service. A feeder can look compliant at the busbar while its farthest customers are well over the limit, which is why solve_time_series() can record every customer’s voltage as well as the busbar’s.

Convergence#

A time-series solve whose inverter control loop fails to converge still reports a solved power flow, with reactive power that means nothing. popo returns convergence as part of every result (SolveReport) rather than leaving it to a log line.

Observability#

A state estimator solves for a voltage magnitude and angle at every bus, less one reference angle: \(2N - 1\) unknowns for \(N\) buses. Each measurement supplies one equation, and each bus known to inject nothing supplies two. Fewer equations than unknowns means no estimate exists at all, whatever the solver; observability() counts them before an estimate is attempted.
