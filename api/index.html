popo.network#

Network models: loading an OpenDSS circuit and inspecting its structure.

Covers the circuit definition rather than its behaviour: buses, lines, transformers, loads and the topology that connects them. Circuit is a handle on the compiled circuit that iterates those as records. Solving is popo.powerflow. OpenDSS is imported lazily, so reading a network definition does not require the solver to be installed.

popo.network.measure_sensitivity(dss, name, base_multiplier=1.0, step_multiplier=2.0)#

Measure how far the worst bus voltage moves when the loading is raised.

The circuit is solved twice under a global load multiplier, so every load scales proportionally and the perturbation does not change the mix of demand across the feeder. Doubling is large enough to be measured above solver tolerance and small enough to remain convergent on the networks this was built for.

Parameters:

  • dss (Any) – An opendssdirect module with a compiled circuit.

  • name (str) – Name to record the measurement under.

  • base_multiplier (float) – Load multiplier of the reference solve.

  • step_multiplier (float) – Load multiplier of the perturbed solve.

Return type:

VoltageSensitivity

Returns:

A VoltageSensitivity.

Raises:

ValueError – If the multipliers are equal, or if raising the multiplier did not raise the substation demand. The second case means the perturbation did not take, and reporting a sensitivity from it would divide by a number near zero and yield an arbitrarily large figure.

popo.network.select_by_utilisation(transformers, screen=None, count=30, random_state=0)#

Choose the transformers to record, held fixed across scenarios.

Given a screening result, the most heavily loaded assets are taken, which is where an operator would look and where a thermal constraint can actually bind. Without one, a uniform sample is drawn and the caller should expect the record to contain few events: on one feeder a random thirty peaked at 0.81 utilisation while the screen had found 250 of 596 above 0.8.

Holding the selection fixed across scenarios is what makes a penetration sweep a comparison of the same assets rather than a fresh draw each time.

Parameters:

  • transformers (DataFrame) – Frame from read_transformers().

  • screen (DataFrame | None) – Optional screening result carrying transformer and utilisation columns, from which peak utilisation is taken.

  • count (int) – Number of transformers to select.

  • random_state (int) – Seed for the uniform fallback, for reproducibility.

Return type:

DataFrame

Returns:

The selected subset of transformers.

Raises:

ValueError – If count is not positive.

popo.powerflow.solve_time_series(dss, transformers, horizon, start_step=0, step_minutes=30, max_control_iterations=200, buses=None)#

Step the circuit, recording each monitored transformer and the feeder head.

Active and reactive power are read at the transformer’s secondary winding, which is what its rating applies to, and voltage at the bus it feeds, which is what the customer sees.

The start step matters on a cooling-dominated feeder. A window in January understates peak loading where demand is summer-peaking, so a probe that cannot start in summer will report that the thermal constraint never binds when it has simply not been asked about the season in which it does.

Parameters:

  • dss (Any) – An opendssdirect module with a compiled circuit.

  • transformers (Sequence[Transformer] | DataFrame) – Monitored transformers, as objects with name, rating_kva and low_voltage_bus, or a frame with those columns.

  • horizon (int) – Number of steps to solve.

  • start_step (int) – Step offset at which to begin, in units of step_minutes.

  • step_minutes (int) – Length of one step.

  • max_control_iterations (int) – Iteration limit for the inverter control loop.

  • buses (Sequence[str] | None) – Buses to record a voltage trace at, e.g. the customer buses from popo.network.residential_loads(). None records none.

Assigns electric vehicles and rooftop generation to customers at a given penetration, with the inverter control the interconnection standard requires, and batteries as a customer-side mitigation. Penetration is the fraction of customers holding the technology, and assignment is drawn from a seeded generator so the same households hold the technology at every level, making a sweep a controlled comparison.

popo.estimation#

State estimation from a sparse measurement set.

Low-voltage networks are largely unobservable: there are measurements at the secondary substation and little downstream. Weighted least squares reconstructs the network state from those few readings plus pseudo-measurements, whose assumed uncertainty is the standing weakness of the method and the thing a calibrated forecast can supply instead.

This is where a multi-target forecast is judged operationally rather than statistically. A distribution operator does not consume a forecast of substation active power, reactive power and voltage directly; it feeds them to an estimator and acts on the network state that comes out. A triple whose marginals are each well calibrated but which is jointly infeasible produces a worse estimate than one that is mutually consistent, and no marginal score can see that.

pandapower is an optional extra, so both modules import it lazily.

popo.profiles#

The SMART-DS (NREL) residential demand pool: paired P and Q profiles.

SMART-DS publishes residential customers with paired active and reactive per-unit profiles at 15-minute resolution for a full year, wired into OpenDSS as mult and qmult on the same LoadShape. Using them, rather than deriving reactive power from an assumed power factor, removes the question of whether a reported P-Q-V coupling is a property of the network or of that assumption: Q comes from the dataset.

The profiles are dimensionless, normalised to a per-customer maximum of one, so they transfer to another network by scaling with that network’s own ratings; what does not transfer is the originating climate’s demand pattern, which is a limitation of any such reuse, not something hidden here.

Draw from this pool with probability proportional to ``n_loads``. A profile is shared by many loads, and the small ones are shared far more widely, so sampling profiles uniformly oversamples rare large customers and inflates apparent transformer loading. Weighted by n_loads the pool reproduces the population mean rating.

popo.profiles.smartds.load_ratings(loads_dir, kind='res')#

Per-customer kW and kvar ratings, read from Loads.dss files.

The two per-unit profiles in load_pool() are each normalised to their own maximum, so a power factor computed from them alone is meaningless. The ratings are what carry the P-to-Q relationship: OpenDSS applies them as P(t) = kW * mult(t) and Q(t) = kvar * qmult(t). Any transfer of these profiles to another network has to carry the rating ratio with them, or the reactive behaviour is lost and an assumed power factor creeps back in.

A profile is reused across many loads with different ratings, so keeping the first match would pick an arbitrary one and bias the pool towards whichever load happens to be read first; the median over a profile’s users is used instead, and the kvar/kW ratio is preserved because it is taken per-load, not assembled from two independent medians.

  • lines at LINE_WIDTH (0.85 pt), interval bands with a BAND_EDGE (0.2 pt) edge, small hollow observation markers;

  • y-axis from set_y_axis(): bottom = (min - eps) rounded down to the data’s round unit, top = (max + eps) rounded up to the tick step, ticks at about four round steps;

  • the legend in one row below the axes (legend_below()).

popo.plotting.line_style.set_y_axis(ax, *arrays, eps_frac=0.04, nbins=4, floor=None, cap=None, unit_interval=False, from_zero=False)#

Y-limits and ticks from the plotted data.

With lo = min - eps and hi = max + eps (eps = eps_frac of the data range), the tick step is a round step giving about nbins intervals; the top is hi rounded up to the step and the bottom lo rounded down to half the step, and ticks sit at the multiples of the step inside the axis, e.g. data 14.6 to 71.4 -> axis 10 to 80, ticks 20, 40, 60, 80.

unit_interval fixes the axis to [0, 1] (ticks 0, 0.25, …, 1) for a rate bounded by 0 and 1 (coverage, NMPI, CWE); from_zero starts the axis at 0 for a non-negative quantity whose natural floor is zero (an error or a width). floor/cap bound the limits otherwise.
